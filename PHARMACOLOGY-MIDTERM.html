Lecture 1 — Pharmacokinetics (ADME)

BCMS-214 · Midterm Revision · Level 2 · 2026–2027

💡 ترجمة المصطلحات: أي مصطلح مهم أو صعب هتلاقي جنبه ترجمته بالعربي (بالشكل ده) عشان المذاكرة تبقى أسهل.
A

Pharmacology & Absorption

الامتصاص

💊 Basics
TopicKey facts
PharmacologyPharmakon (Greek) = drug · -ology = science → the science of the drug: its biochemical & physiological aspects (علم الأدوية). It covers ADME, mechanism of action (آلية التأثير) and toxicity (التسمّم).
2 branchesPharmacokinetics = what the BODY does to the DRUG (movement over time: ADME) · Pharmacodynamics = what the DRUG does to the BODY.
Onset (بداية المفعول)Time from administration to appearance of action → depends on the rate of absorption.
Duration (مدة المفعول)Time from appearance of the effect to its disappearance → depends on the rate of metabolism.
ADME decidesThe route of administration, the amount & frequency of each dose, and the dose intervals.
📥 Absorption
TopicKey facts
DefinitionIrreversible transfer of a drug from its site of administration to the bloodstream (نقل غير عكسي).
Bioavailability (التوافر الحيوي)I.V = complete (100%) → highest. Other routes: only partial.
pHWeak acids (Aspirin) → mostly absorbed from the stomach; weak bases (Amphetamine, Ephedrine) → from the intestine — in both cases because they are unionized there.
Golden ruleUnionized = non-polar = lipid soluble = absorbed · Ionized = polar = NOT absorbed.
IntestineThe major site of absorption for most drugs (even acidic ones): large surface area + high blood flow.
Physical factors↑ blood flow · ↑ surface area · ↑ contact time → ↑ absorption.
🚪 How Drugs Cross Membranes — 5 Mechanisms
TopicKey facts
Passive diffusion (الانتشار البسيط)High → low · no carrier · no energy. Water-soluble drugs pass through aqueous pores; lipid-soluble drugs dissolve in the membrane.
Facilitated diffusion (الانتشار بمساعدة ناقل)High → low · carrier: yes · energy: no (transmembrane carriers).
Active transport (النقل النشط)Against the gradient (low → high) · specific carrier (saturation & competition) · energy: ATP → ADP.
Endocytosis (إدخال للخلية)The cell engulfs the drug and pinches off a drug-filled vesicle · large molecules, e.g. Vitamin B12.
Exocytosis (إخراج من الخلية)Reverse of endocytosis: vesicles release substances out of the cell · Insulin (granules move to the cell membrane → fusion → rupture → exocytosis) · Norepinephrine (stored in membrane-bound vesicles in the nerve terminal → released by exocytosis).
D

Distribution

التوزيع

📤 Distribution
TopicKey facts
DefinitionReversible passage of a drug from the blood to the interstitium (extracellular fluid) and/or the cells of tissues.
Free form (الدواء الحر)Active; can be metabolized & eliminated.
Bound form (albumin)Inactive; not metabolized or eliminated; acts as a reservoir → ↑ duration of action.
Totally freee.g. Lithium: the whole dose reaches the site of action directly → dangerous, hard to recall → dose must be adjusted.
Totally boundCannot reach the site of action (the plasma protein is too large to cross any membrane).
DisplacementSulfonamides / Aspirin (higher affinity to plasma protein) displace other drugs → ↑ free drug: + Warfarin → bleeding (نزيف) · + oral hypoglycemic → hypoglycemic coma (غيبوبة نقص السكر).
Therapeutic indexT.I = LD50 ÷ ED50 (LD50 kills 50% of animals; ED50 treats 50%). ↑ T.I = safer. Narrow T.I: Digitalis · Amphotericin B · Theophylline (any ↑ in free conc. → ↑ toxicity).
M

Metabolism

الاستقلاب

🔬 Metabolism (biotransformation)
TopicKey facts
DefinitionConversion of lipophilic drugs into more polar (water-soluble) compounds so they can be readily excreted. Drugs are eliminated by biotransformation and/or excretion into urine or bile (to feces).
SitesLiver = major site (microsomal enzymes: Cytochrome P450); also kidney & intestines.
Age↓ in the elderly ("exhausted enzymes") and children ("immature enzymes") — Chloramphenicol → Gray baby syndrome (↓ glucuronide conjugation).
SexMales need a higher dose: ↑ metabolism by anabolic hormones (female hormones are catabolic).
Pathological conditione.g. liver diseases (cirrhosis) (تليّف الكبد) → ↓ metabolism.
⚗️ Enzyme Inducers vs Inhibitors
InducersInhibitors
RoleInduction (stimulation) of selected CYP isozymes (تحفيز)Inhibition of selected CYP isozymes (تثبيط)
Metabolism of other drugs↑↓
Plasma level of the other drug↓↑
Effect↓ effect↑ effect → toxicity
Dose of the other drugIncrease (e.g. oral hypoglycemics, oral contraceptives)Decrease (many drugs inhibit CYP pathways of warfarin)
ExamplesRifampin · Carbamazepine · Phenobarbital · PhenytoinOmeprazole · Erythromycin · Ketoconazole · Ritonavir · Cimetidine
E

Excretion

الإخراج

🚰 Excretion
TopicKey facts
Major routeKidney (urine).
Minor routesBile · intestine · lung (some general anesthetics) · sweat · tears · hair (Arsenic in Napoleon's hair) · milk (Penicillin in milk may cause allergy to children).
🫘 Renal Excretion — 3 Steps
StepTypeKey point
1 · Glomerular filtration (الترشيح الكبيبي)PassiveThe drug enters the kidney via the afferent arteriole → glomerular capillary plexus in Bowman's capsule; only the free drug (not bound to albumin) passes through the capillary slits into Bowman's space as part of the glomerular filtrate.
2 · Proximal tubular secretionActiveDrugs not passed into the filtrate go to the efferent arteriole surrounding the PCT and are actively secreted in the proximal tubules (carrier + energy); drugs with similar structure compete for the same carriers.
3 · Distal tubular reabsorptionPassiveMoving toward the distal convoluted tubule, the drug's concentration rises and exceeds that of the perivascular space; if the drug is uncharged, it diffuses back into the systemic circulation.
🧲 Penicillin + Probenecid & Ion Trapping
Details
Penicillin + ProbenecidProbenecid has a higher affinity to the tubular carrier → it is excreted instead; penicillin is retained → longer duration of penicillin.
Ion trapping (احتجاز الأيونات)Adjust urine pH to ↑ the ionized form → less diffusion back to the blood → ↑ clearance. Use the opposite pH to the drug.
Acidify urine (Vit C / NH₄Cl)↑ excretion of BASIC drugs: Ephedrine · Bupivacaine · Pyrimethamine · Amphetamine.
Alkalinize urine (NaHCO₃, sodium bicarbonate)↑ excretion of ACIDIC drugs: Aspirin — useful in barbiturate toxicity (التسمم بالباربيتورات).

📝 Lecture 1 — Cheat Sheet

ملخص منظّم: كل موضوع في كارت لوحده، وتحت كل كارت أكتر غلطة بتتكرر في الامتحان.

🧭 PK basics

  • PK = body → drug (ADME) · PD = drug → body
  • Onset ← absorption rate
  • Duration ← metabolism rate
⚠️ Don't swap onset and duration.

📥 Absorption

  • I.V = 100% (highest bioavailability)
  • Only unionized (lipid-soluble) form is absorbed
  • Weak acid (Aspirin) → stomach · weak base (Amphetamine, Ephedrine) → intestine
  • Intestine = major site: large area + high blood flow
  • ↑ blood flow / surface area / contact time → ↑ absorption
⚠️ Even acidic drugs are mostly absorbed in the intestine.

🚪 Crossing membranes

  • Passive: no carrier, no energy
  • Facilitated: carrier, no energy, along the gradient
  • Active: against the gradient, carrier + ATP, saturable, competition
  • Endo (in): Vit B12 · Exo (out): Insulin, Norepinephrine
⚠️ Facilitated needs a carrier but NO energy.

📤 Distribution

  • Free = active · bound = inactive reservoir (↑ duration)
  • Lithium = totally free → adjust dose
  • Sulfonamides / Aspirin displace: Warfarin → bleeding · oral hypoglycemic → coma
  • T.I = LD50 ÷ ED50 · narrow: Digitalis, Amphotericin B, Theophylline
⚠️ Only the FREE drug is metabolized & eliminated.

🔬 Metabolism

  • Lipophilic → polar · mainly liver (CYP450)
  • Inducers (Rifampin, Carbamazepine, Phenobarbital, Phenytoin): ↑ metabolism → ↓ effect
  • Inhibitors (Omeprazole, Erythromycin, Ketoconazole, Ritonavir, Cimetidine): ↓ metabolism → toxicity
  • ↓ in elderly & children (Chloramphenicol → Gray baby) · males need more (anabolic hormones) · cirrhosis ↓
⚠️ Inducer = lower level; inhibitor = higher level.

🚰 Excretion

  • Kidney = major route
  • Minor: bile, intestine, lung, sweat, tears, hair (Arsenic — Napoleon), milk (Penicillin)
  • Renal steps: filtration (passive) → proximal secretion (active) → distal reabsorption (passive)
⚠️ Only the secretion step is active.

🧲 Interactions & ion trapping

  • Penicillin + Probenecid → penicillin stays longer
  • Ion trapping = use the opposite pH
  • Acidify (Vit C / NH₄Cl) → ↑ excretion of basic drugs
  • Alkalinize (NaHCO₃) → ↑ excretion of acidic drugs (Aspirin)
⚠️ Acid drug → alkaline urine · basic drug → acidic urine.

💡 Memory Hooks
  • Endo = in · Exo = out.
  • Unionized → absorbed. Ionized → not absorbed.
  • Inducer = ↑ enzymes → ↓ drug level → ↓ effect. Inhibitor = ↓ enzymes → ↑ drug level → toxicity.

Receptors & Modes of Action

المستقبلات

🎯 Pharmacodynamics & Receptors
TopicKey facts
Pharmacodynamics (ديناميكا الدواء)Drug–receptor interaction (تفاعل الدواء مع المستقبل): the biochemical & physiological effects of drugs and their mechanism of action.
ReceptorSpecialized target macromolecules on the cell surface or intracellular; usually named after the drug/chemical that interacts best (e.g. histamine receptor).
Drugs act on1 · Receptors: drug (ligand (الرابط)) + receptor → complex → biological response.
2 · Other molecules: Trimethoprim inhibits the enzyme DHFR · Antacid neutralizes heartburn · Activated charcoal adsorbs chemicals.
⚙️ 4 Types of Receptors
TypeMechanismDurationExamples
Ligand-gated ion channelRegulates the flow of ions: Nicotinic receptor + ACh → opens Na⁺ channel → depolarization → skeletal muscle contraction—Nicotinic receptor
GPCRLigand → G-protein GDP → GTP → subunits rearrange → 2nd messenger → response, with signal amplification (تضخيم الإشارة) (↑ intensity & duration)—cAMP: β2 + Epinephrine (adenyl cyclase)
IP3 & DAG: α1, M1, M3 + ACh (phospholipase C → ↑ Ca²⁺ → contraction)
Tyrosine kinase-linked2 domains (extracellular & intracellular); activated → phosphorylates tyrosine residues → modifies target proteinsMinutes to hoursInsulin · Growth hormone · Platelet growth factor
Intracellular nuclearLipophilic drug enters the cell → binds receptor → nucleus → transcription & translation → ↑ specific proteinHours to daysCorticosteroids · Testosterone · Estrogen · Progesterone

Dose-Response Curve

منحنى الجرعة والاستجابة

📈 Graded Dose-Response Curve
TopicKey facts
HyperbolicDrug conc. vs response.
Sigmoidal (S-shape)Log drug conc. vs response → more preferred: linear & more accurate; the EC50 is easier to estimate.
ED50 / EC50Dose (conc.) that produces 50% of the maximal response (Emax). ↓ ED50 → ↑ potency; affinity is an important factor.
1st area↑ dose → no response (very low conc. at the receptor site).
2nd area (middle)↑ dose → ↑ response (linear) — most accurate area for readings.
3rd area↑ dose → constant response (Emax) — all receptors are occupied.

Potency · Efficacy · Affinity

خصائص الدواء

⚖️ Potency vs Efficacy vs Affinity
PotencyEfficacy (intrinsic activity)Affinity
MeaningDrug amount needed for an effect of a given magnitude (50% of max = ED50)Degree to which a drug induces the maximal effect (Emax)Strength of binding of a drug (agonist/antagonist) to a receptor
Depends onAffinityNumber of drug-receptor complexes (only agonists have it; antagonists have none)High dissociation → low affinity (and vice versa)
ComparesDrugs of the same category (same MOA, differ in dose)Drugs of different categories (different MOA)—
ExampleMorphine & Codeine (both narcotic analgesics, relieve post-operative pain): Morphine is 10× more potent → needed in a smaller doseSalicylate (NSAIDs, e.g. Ketorolac, Aspirin) vs narcotics (Morphine), both analgesics in post-operative pain → Morphine has higher efficacy—
⭐ A drug with more efficacy is more therapeutically beneficial than one with more potency.

Agonists & Antagonists

المنبّهات والمضادات

🎯 Agonist
TopicKey facts
DefinitionBinds a receptor & produces intrinsic activity (= efficacy = response). No response if it occupies a single lonely receptor — it must occupy a certain number of receptors.
Types of Agonists
Full (strong)WeakPartial agonist / antagonist
Receptors / doseA little drug gives the max effect, even if not all receptors are occupied (small number of receptors)Needs many more receptors occupied, or a higher doseFails to give the max effect of a full agonist even if all receptors are occupied → submaximal ("weak") response
AffinityHighLowHigh
ExampleAcetylcholine at muscarinic receptorsCodeine (high dose to equal Morphine)—
⛔ Antagonists
TypeHow it works
CompetitiveCompetes with the agonist at the same site (structural similarity with the agonist).
Non-competitive (allosteric)Another site → conformational change (تغيّر في شكل المستقبل) → prevents agonist action.
IrreversibleBinds covalently (permanent) for 24–48 hrs to the active site → fewer receptors available. Ex: Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible α-blocker).
5

Therapeutic Index

مؤشر الأمان

🛡️ Therapeutic Index (Window) = Safety Margin
TopicKey facts
FormulaT.I = LD50 / ED50 (also written TD50 / ED50) (LD50: dose causing toxicity in 50% of the population · ED50: dose giving the therapeutic effect in 50%). Compares the toxicity of 2 drugs.
High T.IWide margin → safer drug · Low T.I = narrow margin → toxic drug.
ApplicationsWarfarin (narrow T.I) → must be hospitalized · Penicillin (wide T.I) · narrow-T.I anticancer drugs are accepted in lethal diseases (Hodgkin's lymphoma) but unacceptable for a simple headache.

📝 Lecture 2 — Cheat Sheet

ملخص منظّم: كل موضوع في كارت لوحده، وتحت كل كارت أكتر غلطة بتتكرر في الامتحان.

🎯 PD & receptors

  • PD = drug–receptor interaction
  • Receptor = target macromolecule (surface or intracellular)
  • Drugs act on receptors or other molecules (Trimethoprim–DHFR, Antacid, Activated charcoal)
⚠️ Some drugs act with NO receptor at all.

⚙️ 4 receptor types

  • Ion channel: Nicotinic + ACh → Na⁺ → contraction
  • GPCR: GDP → GTP, 2nd messenger, amplification · β2 → cAMP · α1/M1/M3 → IP3 & DAG
  • Tyrosine kinase: minutes–hours (Insulin, GH)
  • Nuclear: lipophilic, hours–days (steroids)
⚠️ Nuclear receptors need a lipophilic drug.

📈 Dose-response

  • Sigmoidal (log conc.) preferred: linear & accurate
  • ED50/EC50 = 50% of Emax · ↓ ED50 → ↑ potency
  • Areas: no response → linear (most accurate) → constant (Emax, all receptors occupied)
⚠️ Hyperbolic = conc.; sigmoidal = LOG conc.

⚖️ Potency · Efficacy · Affinity

  • Potency = ED50, depends on affinity, same category (Morphine 10× Codeine)
  • Efficacy = Emax, number of drug-receptor complexes, only agonists, different categories (Morphine > Salicylate)
  • Affinity = binding strength · high dissociation = low affinity
  • Efficacy matters more therapeutically than potency
⚠️ Antagonists have affinity but NO efficacy.

🎯 Agonists

  • Must occupy a certain number of receptors (not a single lonely one)
  • Full: ACh at muscarinic, high affinity
  • Weak: Codeine, low affinity, higher dose
  • Partial: submaximal even if all receptors occupied, high affinity
⚠️ Partial agonist stays submaximal at full occupancy.

⛔ Antagonists

  • Competitive: same site, similar structure
  • Non-competitive (allosteric): another site → conformational change
  • Irreversible: covalent, 24–48 hrs, ↓ receptors (Phenoxybenzamine)
⚠️ Allosteric = a different site.

🛡️ Therapeutic index

  • T.I = LD50 / ED50 · high = safer, low = toxic
  • Warfarin (narrow → hospitalized) · Penicillin (wide)
  • Narrow-T.I anticancer drugs accepted only in lethal disease (Hodgkin's lymphoma)
⚠️ Same formula as in Lecture 1.

Regulates the flow of ions. Nicotinic receptor + ACh → opens the Na⁺ channel → depolarization → skeletal muscle contraction.
GPCR mechanism?
Ligand → activates the G-protein (GDP → GTP) → subunits rearrange → 2nd messenger → response, with signal amplification.
The cAMP pathway?
β2 + Epinephrine → G-protein → Adenyl Cyclase → cAMP → protein phosphorylation.
The IP3 & DAG pathway?
α1, M1 & M3 + ACh → G-protein → Phospholipase C → IP3 & DAG → regulate intracellular calcium → contraction.
Tyrosine kinase-linked receptor?
2 domains; phosphorylates tyrosine residues; minutes to hours. Insulin · Growth hormone · Platelet growth factor.
Intracellular nuclear receptor?
Lipophilic drug enters the cell → binds the receptor → enters the nucleus → transcription & translation → hours to days. Steroid hormones.
Hyperbolic vs sigmoidal curve?
Hyperbolic = conc. vs response. Sigmoidal = log conc. vs response → more preferred: linear & more accurate.
What are ED50 / EC50?
The dose (conc.) producing 50% of the maximal response. ↓ ED50 → ↑ potency.
The 3 areas of the dose-response curve?
1st: ↑ dose → no response.
2nd (middle): linear — most accurate.
3rd: constant response (Emax) — all receptors occupied.
Define potency.
Drug amount needed for a given effect (ED50), dependent on affinity; compares drugs of the same category. Morphine is 10× more potent than Codeine.
Define efficacy (intrinsic activity).
Degree to which a drug induces the maximal effect (Emax); depends on the number of drug-receptor complexes; only agonists have it; compares different categories (Morphine > Salicylate).
Efficacy or potency — which is more therapeutically beneficial?
A drug with more efficacy is more therapeutically beneficial than one with more potency.
Define affinity.
Strength of binding of a drug (agonist/antagonist) to a receptor. High dissociation → low affinity, and vice versa.
What is an agonist?
Binds a receptor & produces intrinsic activity (= efficacy = response). No response if it occupies a single lonely receptor.
Full agonist vs weak agonist?
Full (ACh at muscarinic): a little drug, small number of receptors, high affinity.
Weak (Codeine): needs a higher dose / many more receptors, low affinity.
What is a partial agonist/antagonist?
Fails to produce the max effect of a full agonist even if all receptors are occupied → submaximal response; high affinity.
Competitive antagonist?
Competes with the agonist at the same site; has structural similarity with the agonist.
Non-competitive (allosteric) antagonist?
Binds another site → conformational change in the receptor → prevents agonist action.
Irreversible antagonist?
Binds covalently for 24–48 hrs to the active site → fewer receptors available. Ex: Phenoxybenzamine (irreversible α-blocker).
Therapeutic index?
T.I = LD50 / ED50. High (wide margin) = safer; low (narrow margin) = toxic.
Applications of the therapeutic index?
Warfarin (narrow → hospitalized) · Penicillin (wide). Narrow-T.I anticancer drugs are accepted for lethal diseases (Hodgkin's lymphoma) but not for a simple headache.

💡 Memory Hooks
  • Potency = DOSE (↓ ED50 → ↑ potency) · Efficacy = HEIGHT (Emax).
  • Agonist has efficacy ✔ · Antagonist has no efficacy ✘.
  • Competitive = same site · Non-competitive = another site · Irreversible = covalent, 24–48 hrs.
  • High dissociation → low affinity.
  • GDP → GTP switches the G-protein on.
  • β2 + Epinephrine → Adenyl cyclase → cAMP · α1, M1, M3 + ACh → Phospholipase C → IP3 & DAG.
  • Tyrosine kinase = minutes to hours · Nuclear receptor = hours to days (lipophilic).
  • T.I = LD50 / ED50 — the higher, the safer.
  • Middle area of the curve = accurate · 3rd area = all receptors occupied (Emax).
